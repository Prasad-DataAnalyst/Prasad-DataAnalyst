## 1. Overall: global upstream/midstream capex outlook, digital oilfield and O&G software/data market size, and competitive landscape

### Takeaway
- **Upstream spending is shrinking, but gas and LNG are growing.** Oil investment is falling for a third year in 2026 (below USD 500bn according to the IEA). Gas investment is at a 10-year high (USD 330bn), driven by a wave of LNG projects.
- **Digital spending is small but growing faster than capex.** Rystad's figure is the most credible bottom-up number: E&P companies spent about USD 25bn on digital and AI purchases in 2025, rising to more than USD 35bn by 2030.
- **Market-research firms disagree badly on the size of the "digital oilfield" and "data management" markets.** Estimates run from USD 5.9bn to USD 32bn+, and growth rates from 5% to 17%. Their scope definitions are inconsistent, and several numbers cannot be reconciled with Rystad's.
- **The software market is consolidating around SLB.** In April 2026 SLB agreed to buy S&P Global's upstream desktop portfolio (Kingdom, Petra, Harmony).

### Inferences
- **The market-research totals look inflated.** Several firms put O&G "data management" at USD 26–29bn. That roughly equals Rystad's estimate of *all* E&P digital and AI purchases (USD 25bn). Those totals likely bundle hardware, IT services, telecoms and downstream. The report should anchor on Rystad and Deloitte and present the market-research numbers only as a range, labelled as inconsistent.
- **A plausible serviceable niche is in the low single-digit billions.** Desktop data QC, ETL and analytics tools are a small slice of the USD 25bn. This is an estimate, not a sourced figure.
- **Spend is heading to platforms.** The direction of travel (SaaS on Delfi, Lumi/Tela agentic AI, S&P Titan, OSDU on hyperscalers) is cloud and platform-centric and favours large operators. That leaves a structural gap for:
  - lightweight offline tools for organisations that cannot or will not move data to a vendor cloud;
  - users who need to prepare and validate data *before* it goes into OSDU or other platforms.
- **The SLB–S&P deal may unsettle small US independents.** Kingdom and Petra are long-standing desktop tools for US onshore independents. Moving them under SLB, which is pushing users toward SaaS, may create pricing or roadmap uncertainty for those customers. That is an opening for a free local alternative or complement. This is speculative and depends on SLB's post-close pricing.
- **Cost pressure helps a free tool.** Upstream budgets are flat to down in 2026 and Deloitte describes cost control as the theme. Free tools gain traction under those conditions, but enterprise procurement tends to cut "non-core" experiments.

### Gaps
- Rystad's own 2026 upstream capex total and any Rystad regional split were not found.
- Midstream and LNG capex totals for 2025–2026 are missing (only IEA gas at USD 330bn and qualitative LNG statements).
- Mordor, MarketsandMarkets, Grand View and Precedence figures for O&G software or data management were not retrieved, and their methodologies were not visible (paywalled).
- No market share or revenue data was found for Halliburton Landmark, AspenTech (Emerson), Cognite, Baker Hughes Cordant, Kongsberg or Petrolern-type niche vendors.
- The figure of "7,800 Delfi users" seen in one Quartr summary was unverifiable and is omitted.

## 2. Europe: North Sea maturity and decommissioning, regulatory burden, GDPR/Data Act, sovereign-cloud preference

### Takeaway
- **The North Sea is in managed decline.** Norwegian investment is falling from 2026 and UK spending is shifting toward decommissioning. That makes data work around decommissioning, well abandonment and late-life optimisation a growing workload.
- **EU rules raise data-handling obligations.** The Data Act (from Sept 2025), GDPR and US CLOUD Act concerns are driving sovereign-cloud spending, which suits tools that keep data local.

### Cited Findings

**UK**
- **OEUK Decommissioning Insight 2025 (25 Nov 2025) [F]:**
  - UK Continental Shelf decommissioning spend passed £2bn in 2024 for the first time.
  - Decommissioning could exceed oil and gas capex as early as 2028 "if current investment conditions persist", citing the Energy Profits Levy, delayed licensing and market uncertainty.
  - Decommissioning's share of O&G spend rises from 15% (2024) to more than 30% by the end of the decade.
  - About 2,000 wells to be decommissioned by 2034; 124 were decommissioned in 2024. Wells are almost half of forecast cost.
  - 95,000+ tonnes of subsea infrastructure and 883 km of pipelines to be removed over the next decade.
  - Source: [OEUK](https://oeuk.org.uk/world-class-expertise-underpins-resilient-performance-in-decommissioning-sector/).
- **Annual average [S]:** decommissioning spend averages "almost three billion pounds a year" over the decade. Source: [OEUK search result](https://oeuk.org.uk/?p=169305).

**Norway**
- **Norwegian Offshore Directorate (NOD), Jan 2026 [S]:**
  - 2026 investment about NOK 256bn, down 6.5% on 2025, falling to NOK 209bn by 2030.
  - Production plateau of about 4.1m boe/d until 2027, declining to under 3.5m boe/d by 2030.
  - Gas output 123.1 bcm in 2026 (the 2024 record was 124.2 bcm).
  - About 40 exploration wells in 2026 versus 49 in 2025.
  - Sources: [Baird Maritime/Reuters](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/offshore/drilling-production/norways-oil-and-gas-regulator-says-output-will-fall-towards-2030); [Offshore Magazine](https://www.offshore-mag.com/regional-reports/north-sea-europe/news/55342950/norwegian-offshore-directorate-nod-predicts-steady-lower-exploration-levels-offshore-norway-in-2026).

**Investment and supply shifts**
- **WoodMac [F]:** 2026 upstream cuts expected in Europe and North America. Source: [Brazil Energy Insight](https://brazilenergyinsight.com/2026/01/13/global-upstream-capex-set-to-fall-again-in-2026-amid-low-oil-prices/).
- **Effect of the Hormuz closure (Moore Global) [F]:** the early-March 2026 closure disrupted Qatari LNG to Europe on top of the phase-out of Russian gas. African LNG became a "near-term procurement priority" for Europe. Source: [Moore Global](https://www.moore-global.com/news/africas-41-billion-upstream-oil-and-gas-investment-surge-in-2026/).

**EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) [F]**
- Timeline:
  - Applies from 12 Sep 2025; the unfair-terms rules apply to contracts concluded from that date.
  - From 12 Sep 2026, connected products (including "smart machinery") must make data accessible to users by default.
  - From 12 Jan 2027, cloud and data-processing providers can no longer charge switching fees.
  - From 12 Sep 2027, the unfair-terms rules extend to legacy long-term B2B contracts.
- Obligations on providers:
  - IaaS, PaaS and SaaS providers must remove barriers to switching, including bringing processing back in-house.
  - They must take measures against unauthorised transfers of non-personal data held in the EU.
- Source: [Eubelius](https://www.eubelius.com/en/news/the-eu-data-act-in-force-what-changes-on-12-september-2025); also [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/blogs/digilinks/2024/january/eu--the-data-act---new-rules-on-switching-cloud-services-and-iot-data) [S].
- The Act applies to any business offering products or services in the EU, regardless of where it is based [S]. Source: [WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2025/09/eu-data-act-enters-into-force/).

**GDPR reach**
- Article 3(2)(a) covers non-EU controllers offering goods or services to people in the EU "irrespective of whether a payment … is required" [F]. Source: [GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/).
- Article 27 requires such controllers to designate an EU representative in writing, unless processing is occasional, low-risk and does not involve large-scale special-category data [F]. Source: [GDPR Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/).

**UK data law**
- The UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (2025 c.18) amends UK GDPR, including international transfers (s.85, Schedules 7–9), "recognised legitimate interests" (Schedule 4) and cookies/PECR (s.112) [F]. Source: [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents).

**Sovereign cloud**
- **Gartner [S]:** European spending on sovereign cloud infrastructure is set to triple between 2025 and 2027, with about 20% of new spending shifting workloads from global to local providers. Source: [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/europe-spending-on-sovereign-cloud-infrastructure-to-triple-from-2025-2027-gartner/).
- **AWS European Sovereign Cloud (Jan 2026) [S]:** launched in January 2026, but these entities remain wholly owned by Amazon.com, Inc. No court has definitively resolved whether the CLOUD Act can compel a US parent to hand over data held by a foreign subsidiary. Source: [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/the-european-sovereign-cloud-provider-landscape-in-2026-who-exists-and-what-they-offer/). This is vendor-adjacent content; treat with care.
- **Ifri (2019, dated) [S]:** energy companies risk contradictory obligations under the CLOUD Act and GDPR; most European energy firms then worried more about GDPR non-compliance. Source: [Ifri](https://www.ifri.org/sites/default/files/migrated_files/documents/atoms/files/barichella_us_eu_rivalry_data_protection_2019.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Decommissioning creates data work.** About 2,000 UK wells to abandon by 2034 means legacy well files, surveys and integrity data have to be assembled, validated and handed over to regulators and buyers of late-life assets. Typical holders are small late-life operators and decommissioning contractors, which are cost-sensitive buyers well suited to free desktop tooling.
- **A local-only tool avoids most compliance friction.** Because data never leaves the user's machine, the tool avoids CLOUD Act exposure, Data Act switching and cloud obligations, and most GDPR processor obligations for the *operational* data. GDPR still applies to the *registration* data the distributor collects (see section 8).
- **Regulatory reporting adds load.** EU methane MRV and CSRD reporting add data-preparation burden; not researched here because another researcher covers emissions. Validation and export to regulator templates could be a selling point.

### Gaps
- The Netherlands (Groningen closure, offshore small fields) was not researched (search budget exhausted).
- No survey quantifying European energy companies' on-premise versus cloud preferences was found.
- The EU Methane Regulation, CSRD/ESRS and CBAM were deliberately not researched (another researcher's scope).

## 3. Middle East / GCC: NOC expansion and digital programmes, data localisation, in-country value, procurement

### Takeaway
- **GCC NOCs are the world's most aggressive digital and AI adopters.** ADNOC (with its AIQ joint venture) and Aramco (MetaBrain, an LLM, and very large data centres) are the leaders.
- **Sovereignty rules are tightening.** Saudi Arabia proposed an express data-localisation rule in October 2026. The UAE federal PDPL implementing regulations are still pending, with a 1 January 2027 compliance date.
- **Local-content scoring is a high bar for foreign vendors.** Aramco's IKTVA requirement can reach 66–70%, and ADNOC bidders without an ICV certificate score zero.
- **The 2026 war changed priorities.** It damaged Gulf assets, cut Middle East investment (−1%), and favoured Saudi Arabia and the UAE over Iraq and Kuwait.

  - Parameter counts conflict across sources (70bn versus 250bn).

**Saudi Arabia: data rules**
- **PDPL status [S]:**
  - Fully enforceable since 14 Sep 2024.
  - Transfer regulations amended Sep 2024 to align more closely with GDPR. Without adequacy, transfers need SCCs, BCRs or certification.
  - SDAIA issued a non-binding transfer risk-assessment guideline in Feb 2025.
  - Sources: [Clyde & Co, Mar 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines); [Clyde & Co, Feb 2025](https://clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/02/10-legal-updates-ksa-tech-and-data-laws).
- **Draft PDPL Implementing Regulation amendments (published 8 Oct 2026; consultation 6 Oct to 5 Nov 2026) [F]:**
  - Proposed Article 23 adds an **express requirement to store personal data within KSA**. Transfers would still be allowed if they comply with the PDPL.
  - Proposed Article 34 makes registration with the National Register of Controllers mandatory for, among others, controllers that "transfer or disclose personal data outside Saudi Arabia".
  - Proposed Article 24 requires notifying SDAIA within 72 hours of any breach.
  - Entry into force: 60 days after publication in the Official Gazette.
  - Source: [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl).
- **NCA cybersecurity controls [S]:**
  - ECC-2:2024 removed the explicit in-country data hosting requirement, but localisation may still arise under other rules.
  - CST cloud regulations require government-agency data to use CST-registered, KSA-hosted providers.
  - CCC-2:2024 (cloud controls) is mandatory for government bodies and private operators of critical national infrastructure (CNI).
  - Sources: [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/ksa-movement-in-saudi-arabias-cybersecurity-regulatory-regime); [SecurityWall](https://securitywall.co/blog/ccc-cloud-cybersecurity-controls-saudi-arabia).
  - **Conflicting claim:** one vendor blog says hosting responsibility moved to the National Data Management Office (NDMO) under SDAIA. Source: [Secure.com](https://www.secure.com/blog/compliance/ecc-data-residency).

**UAE: data rules [F]**
- Federal PDPL (Decree-Law 45/2021):
  - Executive Regulations were still unpublished as of February 2026.
  - No adequacy list and no SCCs.
  - Transfers rely on contractual protections, express consent or necessity.
  - "Full compliance is required by 1 January 2027."
- Sector rules: health data must stay in the UAE; financial and payment data has Central Bank localisation rules.
- Free zones: DIFC (amended July 2025, documented adequacy assessments) and ADGM (GDPR-mirroring) run separate regimes.
- Source: [Kayrouz & Associates, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/cross-border-data-transfers-under-uae-law-in-2026).
- **Conflicting claim:** weaker sources say the Executive Regulations were issued as Cabinet Decision 111/2023. Source: [Reconn](https://orbit.reconn.io/uae-pdpl-complete-guide/) [S].

**Qatar: data rules [F]**
- Law No. 13 of 2016 (PDPPL), enforced by the National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs (NCGAA) of the National Cyber Security Agency.
- No controller registration.
- Transfers allowed with consent or for a lawful purpose; controllers "should not … curb trans-border data flow".
- Breach notification within 72 hours.
- Fines up to 5m (about USD 1.4m).
- Source: [DLA Piper Data Protection](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=QA).

**In-country value and procurement**
- **ADNOC ICV:**
  - Since 2018, suppliers must hold an annually certified ICV score. Bidders without a certificate score zero, and technically shortlisted bidders, including technology suppliers, are ranked by ICV [S]. Source: [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/Article/819646/Service-Providers-And-ICV).
  - Certification applies to Tier 1 and Tier 2+ suppliers, with no minimum score needed to obtain a certificate [S]. Source: [Kayrouz](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/uae-icv-certification-requirements-national-icv-program).
  - ADNOC ICV is broader than the others: it counts Emiratisation, UAE-made goods, advanced technology and revenue earned outside the UAE, and bidders must propose how to improve their score [F].
- **Aramco IKTVA [F]:** the required score rose from 35% (2016) to 66% in year one of a recent contract, reaching 70% by year five. Missing it is a "substantial breach".
- **QatarEnergy ICV [F]:** QatarEnergy may withhold payments until the ICV score is met. Qatari national products get a price preference of up to 10%.
- Source for the [F] items above: [Pinsent Masons, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/2025/12/middle-east-oil-gas-contract-negotiations-contractors).
- No ICV or IKTVA criteria specific to software vendors were found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **GCC NOCs are not the prime target as direct buyers.** They are building sovereign AI stacks (AIQ, MetaBrain, Panorama) and procure through ICV/IKTVA-scored tenders. A free, unsupported tool will rarely become an *enterprise standard* there.
- **The GCC opportunity is indirect.** It lies in:
  - local service companies and contractors that must deliver clean data to NOCs;
  - engineers and consultants working on sensitive subsurface data who are barred from using foreign SaaS;
  - universities (KFUPM, Khalifa University and similar);
  - regulators.
- **Offline processing fits the direction of travel.** A tool that processes data entirely offline sidesteps localisation (Saudi Art. 23 draft, CST cloud rules, CCC for CNI) because nothing leaves the device. That is a strong, honest message, but it applies only if the tool truly makes no network calls apart from optional registration and update checks.
- **ICV/IKTVA matter only for paid contracts.** Free distribution avoids procurement scoring. If a paid support tier or enterprise contract is later offered to ADNOC, Aramco or QatarEnergy, a local partner or entity will likely be needed to score ICV/IKTVA.
- **The war raises the value of portable, offline capability.** Damage, outages and remote-working disruption in 2026 favour tools that do not depend on centralised connectivity. This is an inference, not evidenced by a source.

- No ICV/IKTVA scoring rules specific to software or IT vendors were found.
- Kuwait and Oman data protection laws were not researched.

**Nigeria: NDPA 2023 and GAID 2025 [F]**
- **Status:** NDPA assented 13 Jun 2023. GAID issued 20 Mar 2025 and in force 19 Sep 2025, replacing NDPR 2019.
- **Extraterritorial reach:** covers foreign organisations that target Nigerian data subjects, even without a Nigerian presence.
- **Registration as a data controller/processor of major importance (DCPMI):**
  - Ordinary-High: 200 to under 1,000 data subjects in 6 months; N10,000 a year.
  - Extra-High: 1,000 to under 5,000; N100,000; annual compliance audit return due 31 March.
  - Ultra-High: more than 5,000; N250,000; annual compliance audit return.
- **Transfers:** adequacy, or SCCs/BCRs reviewed by the NDPC, or narrow exceptions.
- **Breaches:** notify the NDPC within 72 hours.
- **Fines:** up to the greater of 2% of annual gross revenue or N10m for DCPMIs (N2m for others).
- **AI/IoT:** a DPIA is required before deploying them.
- Source: [DLA Piper Data Protection — Nigeria](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG).
- Compliance audit return filing fees of N100,000 to N1,000,000 apply from the 2026 audit cycle [F]. Source: [DLA Piper Privacy Matters, Jun 2025](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/06/nigeria-ndpc-issues-gaid-key-compliance-insights/).

**Nigeria: local content (NCDMB)**
- NCDMB says it enforces the NOGICD Act so that local service companies are engaged in **all IT-related jobs** in the oil and gas industry [S].
- NCDMB and NITDA formed a joint committee on ICT local content (2020) [S].
- NCDMB and SLB launched an academia programme (2021) to develop indigenous E&P software talent [S].
- An industry figure said fragmented data is a key cost barrier in Nigerian oil and gas [S].
- Sources: [Shipping Position](https://shippingposition.com.ng/ncdmb-nitda-set-up-committee-on-local-content-development-in-oil-gas/); [Sweetcrude Reports](https://sweetcrudereports.com/ncdmb-schlumberger-to-engage-academia-on-oil-gas-software-solutions/); [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/ncdmb-launches-digital-platforms-to-boost-ease-of-doing-business-in-oil-and-gas/).

### Inferences
- **Africa is arguably the strongest fit for a free, offline tool.** Three factors point that way:
  - Licence affordability is a constraint for indigenous operators, especially Nigerian marginal-field and indigenous firms that took over onshore assets from the majors.
  - Remote sites have connectivity limits (not evidenced here; see Gaps).
  - NOCs and regulators (NUPRC, Sonangol/ANPG, NAMCOR, Petrosen) are building national data capacity.
- **Local partners solve the NCDMB question.** NCDMB's "local companies for IT jobs" stance makes paid foreign IT services harder. A free tool used *by* Nigerian service companies, or a partnership with Nigerian firms for training and support, fits the regime.
- **Registration triggers NDPA obligations at a low threshold.** NDPA/GAID reach a foreign distributor collecting Nigerian registrants' data. Collecting data on more than 200 Nigerian data subjects in six months may trigger DCPMI registration if the distributor is deemed "operating in Nigeria"; it is unclear whether a foreign free-software distributor would be classed as a DCPMI. Needs counsel.

## 5. North America: US shale data volumes, independents versus majors, software buying, Canada

### Takeaway
- **US spending is being cut, but the market is large.** US tight-oil spending fell almost 10% in 2025, and WoodMac expects further North American cuts in 2026. Still, the US is the largest single O&G software market.

- **Small operators are most exposed to tool changes.** Thousands of small and mid-size US independents, mineral and royalty firms, and consultancies run desktop workflows on LAS, CSV and production spreadsheets. ### Inferences
- **China is likely a closed market.** Geological and subsurface data is very likely to be treated as sensitive or important data in practice. Chinese NOCs (CNPC, Sinopec, CNOOC) use domestic software stacks, so the market is effectively closed to a foreign-distributed tool, though Chinese universities and researchers may download it. A local-only design is compliant by default because no data is exported, but the registration flow would collect Chinese personal information. Consider whether to accept Chinese registrations, which is a legal and policy choice.
- **India suits free, offline tools.** ONGC and Oil India, a large university and consultancy base, cost sensitivity, and DPDP's power to restrict transfers all point that way. Registration data from Indian users triggers DPDP notice and consent duties from May 2027.
- **Australia and Malaysia are mature basins.** As in the North Sea, decommissioning and late-life optimisation create data-preparation work.

## 8. Distribution considerations for a free download with registration (name, email, country): privacy, export control, sanctions

### Takeaway
- **Collecting name, email and country is enough to bring in privacy law.** It is personal data under every regime reviewed. Several regimes reach foreign distributors that *target* their residents (GDPR, NDPA, DPDP, China's PIPL/NDSMR), even for free software.
- **Export controls are light if the tool is open source.** Publicly available open-source code (including publicly available encryption source code once BIS is notified) is generally outside the US EAR.
- **Sanctions still apply.** Comprehensive embargoes and the EU/UK bans on supplying oil and gas exploration software to Russia make country screening of registrations prudent.

### Cited Findings

**Privacy regimes**
- **EU GDPR [F]:**
  - Applies to non-EU controllers offering goods or services to people in the EU "irrespective of whether a payment … is required" (Art. 3(2)(a)). Source: [GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/).
  - Such controllers must designate an EU representative in writing (Art. 27(1)), unless processing is occasional, not large-scale special-category, and unlikely to result in risk (Art. 27(2)). Source: [GDPR Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/).
- **UK [F]:** the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amends UK GDPR on international transfers (s.85, Schedules 7–9), recognised legitimate interests (Schedule 4) and cookies (s.112). Source: [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents).
- **Saudi PDPL:**
  - Enforceable since 14 Sep 2024 [S]. Source: [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/saudi-pdpls-first-anniversary-amendments-enforcement-signals-and-whats-next/).
  - The draft Oct 2026 amendments would add in-Kingdom storage, mandatory controller registration for those transferring data abroad, and 72-hour breach notice [F]. Source: [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl).
- **UAE PDPL [F]:** Executive Regulations pending; transfers rely on contract, express consent or necessity; full compliance by 1 Jan 2027. DIFC and ADGM have their own regimes. Source: [Kayrouz](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/cross-border-data-transfers-under-uae-law-in-2026).
- **Qatar PDPPL [F]:** no registration; 72-hour breach notice; fines up to 5m. Source: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=QA).
- **Nigeria NDPA/GAID [F]:** extraterritorial for those targeting Nigerian data subjects; DCPMI registration from 200 data subjects in six months; fines up to the greater of 2% of revenue or N10m. Source: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=NG).
- **India DPDP [F]:** notice and consent duties and the transfer rule apply from 13 May 2027; covers digital personal data only. Source: [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-).
- **California CCPA [F]:** thresholds are revenue over USD 25m, 100,000+ California consumers, or 50%+ of revenue from selling data. Non-profits are generally exempt. Covered businesses need a notice at collection, rights to delete and opt out, and 45-day response times. Source: [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa).
- **China [F]:** the NDSMR reach overseas processing that provides products or services to individuals in China. Source: [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/prc-network-data-security-management-regulations).

**Export controls (US EAR) [F]**
- Publicly available encryption source code (for example, open source available free online) is not subject to the EAR once the email notification under 15 CFR 742.15(b) is sent.
- Object code is also outside the EAR if its corresponding source code is publicly available and notified.
- Calling or incorporating open-source code does not by itself make a combined item publicly available; the item must be evaluated as a whole.
- Mass-market encryption object code properly self-classified as 5D992.c falls outside the EAR once made publicly available, for example as a free app.
- Source: [BIS — Encryption items not subject to the EAR](https://www.bis.gov/learn-support/encryption-controls/encryption-items-not-subject-to-ear).
- A new BIS notification is needed if the posting URL changes [S]. Source: [magicsplat summary of 742.15(b)](https://www.magicsplat.com/blog/ear/).

**Sanctions**
- **OFAC [F]:** 36 active programmes, including Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia-related (Russian Harmful Foreign Activities; Ukraine-/Russia-related), Belarus, Venezuela-related, Syria-related PAARSS, and others. The page does not label which are comprehensive embargoes. Source: [OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information).
- **EU Russia sanctions:**
  - Since 18 Dec 2023, the EU bans supplying enterprise-management (ERP) and design/manufacturing software to the Russian government and Russia-established entities [S]. Sources: [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102ivvo/the-eu-adopts-12th-package-of-sanctions-against-russia); [EC FAQ](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-02/faqs-sanctions-russia-software_en%2C.pdf).
  - The 16th package (Feb 2025) added **oil and gas exploration software** [S]. Source: [Estonian Tax & Customs Board](https://emta.ee/en/news/eu-adopts-16th-package-sanctions-against-russia-and-belarus).
- **UK Russia sanctions (April 2025) [S]:** Schedule 3IA restricts business-enterprise, industrial-design and **oil and gas software** (exploration, production, reservoir simulation, drilling and completion), including via SaaS, to persons connected with Russia or for use in Russia. Sources: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/global-sanctions-alert/2025/new-uk-trade-sanctions-on-russia-restrict-transfers-of-software-and-technology); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/04/new-uk-sanctions-targeting-russia).
  - Counsel describe the UK lists as broadly similar, though not identical, to EU and US restricted-software lists.

### Inferences
Inferences for a registration-gated free download; to be validated by counsel.

- **Data minimisation:**
  - Collect only name, email and country, plus optional organisation type for market sizing.
  - State the purpose (licensing/registration, product updates, optional marketing with separate opt-in).
  - Publish a privacy notice that covers GDPR Art. 13-type disclosures, NDPA/GAID's "simple, conspicuous" policy requirement, and DPDP notice requirements (from May 2027).
  - Store registration data with a reputable processor under a DPA and SCCs.
- **Representatives and registration:**
  - If EU and UK registrants are numerous, consider an EU Art. 27 representative and a UK representative. The Art. 27(2) "occasional, low-risk" exception may or may not apply to a continuous registration programme.
  - Saudi registration and localisation (if the October 2026 draft is adopted) could apply to any controller transferring Saudi residents' data abroad. Monitor the outcome of the 5 Nov 2026 consultation.
  - Nigeria DCPMI registration thresholds are low (200 data subjects in six months).
- **Breach readiness:** 72-hour regulator-notification rules appear in GDPR, NDPA, Qatar and the Saudi draft, so an incident-response plan is needed even for a small registration database.
- **Export control:**
  - Releasing the workbench as genuinely open source (public repository) puts the source and corresponding binaries in the "publicly available" category.
  - If the tool includes encryption beyond standard OS or HTTPS calls, for example encrypted project files, send the 742.15(b) email notification to BIS and the ENC Encryption Request Coordinator.
  - If any closed-source binary component is distributed, self-classify as 5D992.c mass market.
- **Sanctions:**
  - Even "publicly available" software does not automatically remove OFAC or EU/UK sanctions exposure for *services* such as registration, support and updates.
  - The EU and UK explicitly ban oil and gas exploration and production software to Russia.
  - Prudent practice: use the country field to refuse registrations from comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, the Russia-occupied Ukrainian regions) and from Russia and Belarus for O&G software, and screen against denied-party lists if offering paid support.
  - Syria's status changed in 2025, which I could not verify (see Gaps).
- **Where the distributor is established matters most.** UK, EU, Indian or US establishment determines the baseline regime and which sanctions apply directly. The workspace owner may be India-based (not verified), which would also bring DPDP into play for the distributor's own processing.
