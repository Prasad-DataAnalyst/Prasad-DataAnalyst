## 1. Custody transfer data workflow: data produced, standards, energy calculation, actors, report exchange

### Takeaway
Ship-to-shore LNG custody transfer is still a *static, ship-tank-based* measurement. An opening and a closing custody transfer survey (CTS) record tank level, temperature and pressure (plus trim and list) on board. A line sample analysed by GC gives composition, and density and GCV are calculated from that composition. Energy = V × D × GCV − displaced vapour energy ± gas burned in the engine room. An independent surveyor then issues the final quantity report. The governing reference is the GIIGNL LNG Custody Transfer Handbook, 6th edition (May 2021); no 7th edition was found. The ISO standards are ISO 10976:2023 (on-board measurement), ISO 6976 (calorific value, current edition 2016), ISO 8943 (sampling) and ISO 6974 (GC). Reference-condition and unit conventions differ by region: MMBtu in international trade, kWh at 25/0 °C at EU terminals.

### Cited Findings
**Energy formula and definitions**
- The GIIGNL-derived general formula is **E = (V_LNG × D_LNG × GCV_LNG) − E_gas displaced ± E_gas to ER**. E is net energy transferred, "most frequently expressed in millions of British Thermal Units" (MMBtu). V is LNG volume loaded or unloaded (m³), D is LNG density (kg/m³) and GCV is gross calorific value (MMBtu/kg). E_gas displaced is the energy of vapour returned to shore or ship, or of gas that enters the tanks during unloading. E_gas to ER is gas consumed in the engine room, including the gas combustion unit (GCU): it is added for loading and subtracted for unloading — [sea-man.org LNG measurement (summarising GIIGNL CTH)](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- GCV (gross) is the standard basis for international trading. Some power generators prefer net calorific value, which is about 10% lower — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Density is *calculated*, not measured. It comes from average composition, average temperature and NBS (Klosek-McKinley) coefficients. GCV is calculated from average composition and component reference tables — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Displaced gas may be sampled and analysed by GC, or an assumed fixed composition can be used (e.g., pure methane) — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)

**Measurement sequence and data points**
- The opening CTS takes place after the loading arms are connected and before the ship's manifold valves open. The closing CTS takes place after the arms are drained and inerted and the manifold valves are closed. Volume transferred = closing volume − opening volume, and piping must be in the same inventory state at both surveys — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Level readings are the arithmetic average of several successive readings taken at regular intervals. They are corrected for trim, list and temperature, with millimetres as the smallest unit. Gas flow to the engine room is normally stopped and its valves sealed during transfer unless the parties agree otherwise — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Instruments: capacitance and radar level gauges are primary and float gauges secondary (LIDAR is rare). Temperature probes are spread over the tank height, and tank pressure, trim and list are also measured. Gauge tables, trim/list tables and contraction tables (for Moss/SPB tanks) are applied. Coriolis and ultrasonic flow meters are "not yet conventional" for custody transfer because proving methods are lacking — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Sampling is continuous or discontinuous in the transfer line and must happen *before* flashing in the ship's tanks — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)

**Standards**
- Standards cited by the handbook include ISO 6976 (GCV), ISO 6974-6 (GC), ISO 8943 (LNG sampling), ISO 16664 (calibration gases), ISO 15112 (energy determination), ISO 12213 (compression factor), ISO 18132-1 (on-board level gauges), ISO 10976 (cargo measurement on LNG carriers), ISO 13398 (custody transfer on board ship) and ISO 28460 (ship–shore interface). GPA alternatives are GPA 2261, GPA 2145, GPA 2172 and HM 21. Uncertainty follows JCGM 100:2008 (GUM) and JCGM 200:2012 (VIM). **Mixing ISO and GPA references is discouraged** — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- **GIIGNL Custody Transfer Handbook, 6th edition (2021)** is the latest edition listed on GIIGNL's site. It "reflects GIIGNL's understanding of best current practice at the time of publication" — [GIIGNL resource page](https://www.giignl.org/resources/custody-transfer-handbook-6th-edition-2021)
- The 6th edition (May 2021) re-emphasises ISO 10723 GC performance evaluations as best practice. It requires calibration "with a CRM or better", forbids methane-balance verification gases, and introduces **Raman spectroscopy** for direct in-line LNG composition analysis — [EffecTech summary](https://www.effectech.co.uk/?p=2947)
- The 5th edition (2017, led by Fluxys LNG) added partial (un)loading, reloading at import terminals, ship-to-ship transfer and small ship-to-shore operations. It states it is "neither a standard nor a specification" — [GIIGNL 2017 release](https://giignl.org/?p=5602); [EffecTech 5th ed.](https://www.effectech.co.uk/giignl-lng-custody-transfer-handbook-5th-edition-performance-evaluations-specified-as-best-practice/)
- **ISO 10976:2023** ("Refrigerated light hydrocarbon fluids — Measurement of cargoes on board LNG carriers") is the 3rd edition, published Aug 2023 and replacing the 2015 edition. It covers liquid and vapour volume, temperature, pressure and total cargo quantity, describes on-board measurement systems and sets general requirements for ship and shore parties. It is maintained by ISO/TC 28/SC 5 — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/83687.html); [Genorma](https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-10976-2023)
- ISO 6976:2016 is the current method for calculating calorific value, density and Wobbe index from molar composition (open-source implementations exist, e.g., the R package ISO6976.2016) — [CRAN ISO6976.2016](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/ISO6976.2016/readme/README.html)
- European terminals quote calorific values per ISO 6976 with **combustion at 25 °C and metering at 0 °C** (101.325 kPa): Gasum Pori terminal, GAZ-SYSTEM (Świnoujście) LNG quality certificate and Conexus Baltic Grid — [Gasum Pori Annex 1](https://www.gasum.com/globalassets/files/pdf/terminals/pori/pori-terminal-rules-annex-1-lng-quality-specifications.pdf); [GAZ-SYSTEM LNG quality certificate](https://gaz-system.pl/dam/jcr:4854ad28-4d3c-4e93-a9e8-9862d86fe331/PX-OI-30-1-F-5_rev7-0_LNG_quality_certificate.pdf); [Conexus](https://conexus.lv/natural-gas-parameters)
- Conversion factors differ between sources. An Estonian regulator annex citing the IGU pocketbook gives 1 t LNG = 53.38 MMBtu (gas at 0 °C, 1 atm), while a commercial site gives 52.14 MMBtu/t — [Konkurentsiamet annex 13](https://www.konkurentsiamet.ee/sites/default/files/konsultatsioonid/annex_13_-_conversion_table_-_30_sep_2022.pdf); [henergy](https://henergy.com/conversion)
- Platts standardised its MMBtu-to-GJ factor at **1.055056**, effective 24 Aug 2026 — [S&P Global Platts subscriber note](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/pricing-benchmarks/our-methodology/subscriber-notes/082426-platts-standardizes-mmbtu-gig-conversion-factor)

**Who does what**
- **Responsibilities depend on the Incoterm.** Under FOB/FAS the buyer provides and maintains the on-board volume, temperature and pressure systems and the seller provides terminal sampling and gas analysis. Under CIF/CFR/DES the roles are reversed, and each party may verify the other's systems — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- The **independent surveyor** is nominated and agreed by both parties. They witness and verify certificates, perform the energy calculation and issue the final quantity report, acting as a mutual representative who should not consult for either party during a dispute. The ship's cargo officer and the terminal representative attend the CTS, and the buyer's representative may attend — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- **Intertek** offers LNG custody transfer inspection "including reporting of energy calculations", ship and shore LNG tank calibration, composition and impurity testing, and ISO 17025 on-site labs — [Intertek LNG services](https://intertek.com/petroleum/testing/lng-services)
- **SGS** operates a portable LNG sampler and testing rig built to the ISO 8943 sampling method with an ABB GC (N₂, CO₂, C1–C6+), which reports composition and calorific value. It is used to independently verify a terminal's sampling system and on-board quality — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/lng-portable-sampler-and-testing-rig)
- An SGS Egypt article (Apr 2026) describes quantity (tank gauging, temperature, density) and quality (composition, N₂, calorific value, impurities) inspection for custody transfer at Egyptian terminals — [SGS Egypt 2026](https://www.sgs.com/en-eg/news/2026/04/lng-inspection-services-in-egypt-ensuring-accuracy-in-custody-transfer-operations)

**Documents and data exchange formats**
- Documents named in the handbook workflow: opening and closing CTS records (level, temperature, pressure) and the volume report; GC and reference-gas calibration certificates; calibration certificates for level, pressure, temperature, trim/list and gas meters; optional calculation-software certificates; gassing-up and cool-down tables; Bills of Lading; and the final quantity report from the independent surveyor — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- The Kongsberg K-Gauge CTS logs tank level, temperature and pressure, computes volumes and "automatically generates a CTS loading-/unloading report; the official document used for purchasing the gas transported." Reports print automatically when the CTS function starts or stops, and custom report templates can be stored and recalled — [Kongsberg CTS](https://www.kongsberg.com/maritime/products/tank-gauging-and-measurment-systems/custody-transfer-system)
- Emerson's Rosemount marine CTS uses dual radar in a single still pipe — [Emerson](https://emerson.com/cs-cz/industries/automation/marine/marine-systems-solution/custody-transfer-systems)
- Yokogawa's unmanned small-scale LNG station keeps daily dispense logs as **CSV files** and compiles an end-of-day report file — [Yokogawa app note](https://yokogawa.com/tw/library/resources/application-notes/automatic-custody-transfer-system-for-unmanned-lng-station)

### Inferences
- The data for each cargo is *small but high-stakes*. A CTS is a few dozen to a few hundred readings: 4–5 tanks × (several level readings + 5–8 temperature probes + pressure + trim/list), opening and closing. A GC composition has about 10–12 components, plus calibration certificates. **Illustrative arithmetic (researcher's assumptions, not sourced):** a 155,000 m³ cargo at ~450 kg/m³ ≈ 69,750 t, and at 53.38 MMBtu/t that is ≈ 3.7 million MMBtu. At an assumed $10/MMBtu that is ≈ $37 million, so a 0.1% energy discrepancy ≈ $37k for that one cargo. This is the economic case for independent recalculation and validation tools.
- No single format dominates the exchange chain. On-board systems print or produce vendor-format reports, terminals hold data in DCS/historian/TMS systems, and surveyors issue signed reports. The workbench-relevant reality is likely *PDF certificates + Excel recalculation sheets + vendor CTS printouts*, which inference supports but no source quantifies (see Gaps).
- An offline desktop tool that implements ISO 6976:2016 (and the 1995 edition still in older SPAs), Klosek-McKinley density, ISO 10976 corrections and configurable reference conditions (25/0, 15/15, 60°F, 0/0) would cover the core recalculation that surveyors and buyers' measurement teams perform.

### Gaps
- No **7th edition** of the GIIGNL CTH was found. The 6th (2021) appears to be current as of Oct 2026, but GIIGNL's document library could not be fetched directly (404s on PDFs).
- **EN/ISO 12838** (LNG sampling system suitability testing) could not be verified in this session. Background knowledge, unverified here: EN 12838 is the European standard for suitability testing of LNG sampling systems and is referenced in GIIGNL CTH.
- **Overall energy-measurement uncertainty** figures (background, unverified: GIIGNL commonly cites roughly ±0.5% to ±1% for ship-tank-based energy determination) could not be confirmed from a primary source.
- **Saybolt (Core Laboratories)** LNG service pages were not returned by searches.
- No source quantified how custody transfer reports are exchanged (PDF vs Excel vs portal/EDI). This is an inference only.

## 2. Boil-off gas (BOG) data and reporting, cargo quality and composition records, demurrage/laytime data

### Takeaway
BOG matters commercially in two places. At custody transfer it is the "gas to engine room" and displaced-vapour terms. Under charterparties it is the guaranteed maximum daily boil-off, where excess BOG is typically deducted from hire. Both require voyage-level reconciliation of CTS figures, fuel-gas consumption, heel and reliquefaction data, and disputes centre on calculation basis (100% vs ~98% capacity), exclusions (nitrogen, warm cargo, weather) and evidence. Demurrage and laytime data are handled alongside these, but very little public detail exists for them.

### Cited Findings
- Charterparties typically guarantee a boil-off rate "usually no more than **0.15% per day** of the vessel's total cargo capacity". Losses beyond it are typically deductible from hire, and the actual rate can only be set after delivery and several laden voyages — [Steamship Mutual](https://www.steamshipmutual.com/publications/articles/lng0805)
- GTT distinguishes *guaranteed boil-off* (charterparty volume difference between loading and unloading over a voyage, dependent on composition and terminal conditions) from *boil-off rate* (design performance of the containment system) — [GTT Inside, Feb 2019](https://gtt.fr/sites/default/files/gtt_inside_14_february_2019.pdf)
- Gard (26 Nov 2025): the calculation basis is contested. 100% tank volume favours owners, while cargo capacity (~98%) favours charterers. Owners seek exclusions for warm terminal LNG, backloading, high nitrogen, low cargo quantities and sloshing in severe weather, and should have a right to sample cargo at loading to evidence nitrogen or heavy-hydrocarbon issues. Reliquefaction changes the deduction logic, and under ShellLNGtime excess boil-off is excluded from fuel consumption (clause 29d disapplies Hague-Visby for boil-off provisions). Gard also notes that "emissions responsibilities are likely to appear more often in charterparty terms" — [Gard Insights](https://www.gard.no/en/insights/when-cargo-becomes-fuel-complexities-of-boil-off-and-reliquefaction-in-lng)
- A TotalEnergies–owner charter amendment filed with the SEC makes an agreed **Boil-Off Calculation Procedure** the *sole remedy* for maximum daily boil-off claims — [SEC Exhibit 4.26.5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1603016/000110465920042718/tm206799d1_ex4-265.htm)
- A Pertamina LNG carrier tender term sheet sets separate laden and ballast boil-off guarantees and ties heel remaining at discharge (above initial heel) to a reduction of the ballast bonus at an LNG price estimate — [Pertamina term sheet](https://www.pertamina.com/Media/Upload/Invitation-Prebid/Term-Sheet-LNG-Vessel-Procurement-Pertamina.pdf)
- Charterer voyage instructions can push a vessel past its maximum boil-off rate, e.g., "burn only boil-off while maintaining average speed" — [Gard](https://www.gard.no/en/insights/when-cargo-becomes-fuel-complexities-of-boil-off-and-reliquefaction-in-lng)
- **Regulatory BOG and methane data for ships:** methane slip is reported under EU MRV and from **2026 falls within EU ETS**, so allowances must be surrendered for CH₄ and N₂O as well as CO₂. FuelEU Maritime (first reporting period 2025) counts methane slip on a well-to-wake basis. Default slip factors apply unless measured — [Manifold Times/DNV](https://www.manifoldtimes.com/news/dnv-eu-guideline-for-reporting-and-verifying-actual-methane-slip-for-fueleu-eu-mrv-ets/); [Normec](https://certification.normecgroup.com/news/eu-ets-2026-what-operators-need-to-know-about-full-compliance-and-rising-costs)
- Default slip values are reported as ranging from 3.1% (dual-fuel medium-speed) to 0.2% (dual-fuel slow-speed diesel). A Gasum article lists different engine-type figures (e.g., 7% for slow-speed Otto dual-fuel), **so the sources conflict** — [Manifold Times/DNV](https://www.manifoldtimes.com/news/dnv-eu-guideline-for-reporting-and-verifying-actual-methane-slip-for-fueleu-eu-mrv-ets/); [Gasum 2026](https://www.gasum.com/en/news-and-customer-stories/articles/2026/eu-ets-and-fueleu-maritime-the-double-dividend-of-lng-compliance-in-2026/)
- The European Commission issued guidelines (Oct 2025) for reporting and verifying *actual* (measured) methane slip, and ClassNK's EU ETS FAQ (3rd ed., Feb 2026) explains how a certified measured slip factor replaces defaults — [Manifold Times](https://www.manifoldtimes.com/news/dnv-eu-guideline-for-reporting-and-verifying-actual-methane-slip-for-fueleu-eu-mrv-ets/); [Maritime Executive/ClassNK](https://maritime-executive.com/corporate/classnk-releases-faqs-on-the-eu-ets-for-shipping-3rd-edition)
- A 2026 single-ship study found measured slip coefficients of 1.34% (IMO method) and 1.36% (FuelEU method), well below defaults — [JMSE 14:1315 (MDPI)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jmse/jmse-14-01315/article_deploy/jmse-14-01315.pdf)
- **Quality and composition records:** the SGS rig reports N₂, CO₂ and C1–C6+ plus calorific value. Terminal quality certificates (e.g., GAZ-SYSTEM) give composition, GCV, Wobbe and density at stated reference conditions — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/lng-portable-sampler-and-testing-rig); [GAZ-SYSTEM certificate](https://gaz-system.pl/dam/jcr:4854ad28-4d3c-4e93-a9e8-9862d86fe331/PX-OI-30-1-F-5_rev7-0_LNG_quality_certificate.pdf)
- Quality must be tracked through liquefaction and regasification to meet FERC and other rules ("Each step of the value chain introduces risks that will degrade quality") — [Emerson in Gas Processing & LNG, Mar 2024](https://www.gasprocessingnews.com/articles/2024/02/navigating-the-complex-lng-value-chain)
- **Demurrage:** terminal software must schedule dock events and *calculate demurrage*, and cargo scheduling, demurrage and truck/rail transfer data are often spread across spreadsheets — [Emerson, Gas Processing & LNG](https://www.gasprocessingnews.com/articles/2024/02/navigating-the-complex-lng-value-chain)

### Inferences
- A single voyage produces several overlapping BOG datasets: CTS figures at the load port, CTS figures at discharge, daily noon reports (fuel gas, GCU, reliquefaction), the EU MRV/ETS methane-slip calculation and the charterparty boil-off calculation. These rarely sit in one system. Reconciling them, and producing audit-ready evidence for hire deductions or ETS verification, is a natural workbench use case.
- From 2026, EU ETS methane coverage and FuelEU penalties put a direct monetary value on per-voyage methane data. This raises demand for verifiable calculation trails on LNG carriers as well as on terminals.

### Gaps
- No public, LNG-specific source on **laytime/demurrage data practices** (e.g., allowed laytime under LNG SPAs, NOR/time-sheet handling, demurrage claim volumes) was obtained before the search budget ran out. Background, unverified: LNG SPAs typically set allowed laytime of about 24–48 hours with demurrage rates indexed to ship size or charter rates, and laytime disputes centre on NOR validity, berth congestion and terminal-caused delays.
- No quantitative data on how many BOG or hire-deduction disputes occur per year.
- No source found on cargo-quality dispute frequency (e.g., off-spec N₂, Wobbe out of range at European terminals).

## 3. Methane/GHG intensity per cargo: buyer and regulator requirements, SGE per cargo, required data sources

### Takeaway
Per-cargo emissions disclosure is moving from voluntary to regulated. The pieces are: the GIIGNL MRV & GHG Neutral Framework (2021) cargo statement; supplier-issued tags such as Cheniere's CE Tags (since 2022); MiQ certification of the LNG supply chain; and the JERA/KOGAS-led CLEAN supplier questionnaires. The **EU Methane Regulation (EU) 2024/1787** is the binding driver: importer reporting since 2025, MRV equivalence from 1 Jan 2027, intensity reporting from Aug 2028 and an intensity cap from 2030. However, on **13 Oct 2026 the Commission proposed a one-year delay** to the importer obligations, citing the Hormuz crisis and energy security. The core data problem is *producer-level traceability* for portfolio/FOB LNG, especially US cargoes.

### Cited Findings
**EU Methane Regulation (EUMR) — import side**
- Phases as summarised by Orrick (June 2026): **Phase 1 (from 5 May 2025)**: importers supply exporter/producer information, methane monitoring measures and third-party verification, reporting annually by 31 May. **Phase 2 (from 1 Jan 2027)**: importers must show imports are subject to EU-equivalent MRV for contracts concluded after 4 Aug 2024, with "all reasonable efforts" for older contracts; first report due 31 May 2027; penalties up to 20% of annual turnover. **Phase 3 (from 5 Aug 2028)**: report methane intensity of imports using a methodology expected around Aug 2027; first report 31 May 2029. **Phase 4 (from 5 Aug 2030)**: new or renewed contracts must not exceed a methane-intensity threshold that has not yet been set — [Orrick, June 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/06/Uncertain-times-for-the-EU-Methane-Regulation)
- Status in June 2026: implementing acts, standards and methodologies were "not yet developed or notified", the Methane Transparency Database was scheduled for Sept 2026, several member states had not nominated competent authorities, and a leaked draft suggested non-binding guidance recommending penalty waivers until 2029. On 26 June 2026 more than half of member states at the TTE Council asked for a *legal* postponement of three years — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/06/Uncertain-times-for-the-EU-Methane-Regulation)
- An HSF Kramer briefing (Aug 2026) is headlined "EU Commission clarifies compliance options and recommends three-year penalty grace period". Its content could not be fetched (403) — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2026-08/eu-methan-regulation-eu-commission-clarifies-compliance-options-and-recommends-three-year-penalty-grace-period)
- **13 Oct 2026:** the Commission adopted a legislative proposal for a **one-year delay** of importer MRV obligations (gas, oil, coal) and of methane-intensity reporting provisions for importers and EU producers. Commissioner Jørgensen cited protection from price volatility, and the Commission spokesperson said "We are not currently facing a supply crisis" but the situation is "very volatile". The proposal goes to Parliament and Council — [Eunews, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/10/14/methane-commission-proposes-a-one-year-delay-to-import-rules/); [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/europe/20261014/22b1f49944054239ab8a98317053107b/c.html)
- Reporting says the proposal would move import obligations to **January 2028**, with public feedback open until 6 Nov — [bne IntelliNews (search excerpt; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.intellinews.com/eu-adopts-proposal-to-postpone-eu-methane-regulations-by-one-year-476877)
- An LNG trade-group leader said one year "is not enough time" and urged a three-year delay — [NGI (search excerpt)](https://naturalgasintel.com/news/eu-methane-rule-delay-buys-little-time-no-certainty-for-us-lng/)
- In April 2026 the Commission was reported to be "eyeing a country-level standard" for equivalence (headline only) — [S&P Global, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/040926-ec-eyeing-country-level-standard-for-eu-methane-regulation)
- Equivalence routes: producers meeting EU quantification rules **or OGMP 2.0 Level 5** with independent third-party verification, or Commission recognition of a country's regime. Data is required "at the level of the producer". Verifiers must be independent (Art. 9), and the pool of qualified verifiers is limited. The intensity methodology must resolve associated-gas allocation (mass vs energy vs economic). Parliament's proposed ≤0.2% intensity cap was dropped from the Nov 2023 deal — [CSIS, May 2024](https://www.csis.org/analysis/eu-methane-rules-impact-global-lng-exporters)
- Many US LNG sellers buy rather than produce gas, and much US LNG is sold FOB to aggregators selling from portfolios. Qatar's concentrated production makes compliance easier — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/eu-methane-rules-impact-global-lng-exporters)
- Resellers and aggregators sourcing from pools or hubs struggle to trace volumes to production sites, and Noerr argues some EU importers may have to stop imports to avoid fines — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/eu-methane-regulation-creates-new-challenges-for-the-lng-industry)
- FOB sellers may resist EUMR cost or risk, buyers may avoid or resell EU-bound cargoes, and EUMR-compliant and non-compliant supply may price differently — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/06/Uncertain-times-for-the-EU-Methane-Regulation)
- Industry is split on reading Art. 28(1): the equivalence obligation may apply to contracts after 4 Aug 2024 even though reporting starts in 2027 — [Baker Botts, Nov 2024](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2024/november/the-european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-the-importer-requirements-of-the-eu-methane)

**GIIGNL MRV & GHG Neutral Framework**
- Launched Nov 2021, it sets an independently verified **Cargo Statement** of emissions and offsets for declaring "GHG-neutral" cargoes. It covers the full chain (extraction → liquefaction → shipping → regas → end use), requires methane to be reported **separately** alongside all seven Kyoto gases and lets value-chain owners use primary site-specific data — [GIIGNL release](https://giignl.org/?p=6938); [LNG Prime](https://lngprime.com/?p=34068); [Offshore Energy](https://offshore-energy.biz/?p=505245)
- Claiming neutrality requires a verified full life-cycle footprint, a long-term decarbonisation commitment, a reduction plan and high-quality offsets. It is aligned with PAS 2060:2014 and does not replace existing GHG calculation standards — [Executive summary (Shell-hosted)](https://fourleafdigital.shell.com/webapps/lng-decarbonisation-pathway/images/docs/executive-summary-GIIGNL-framework.pdf)

**Supplier per-cargo statements**
- **Cheniere Cargo Emission Tags (since 2022):** CO₂e on absolute and intensity bases plus methane intensity (as a % of LNG delivered), FOB (through liquefaction) or DAP (through shipping). They come from a peer-reviewed LCA model and include producer-level methane intensity. SGS gave limited-assurance verification of the LCA model under ISO 14064-3:2019, consistent with ISO 14067:2018. Cheniere calls the tags an estimate, "not a precise measurement" — [Cheniere CE Tags](https://www.cheniere.com/our-responsibility/climate/ce-tags); [Cheniere 2021 release](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/214/cheniere-to-provide-cargo-emissions-data-to-lng-customers)
- Pavilion Energy's supply deal with Chevron requires the supplier to document each cargo's carbon footprint — [Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2190361-cheniere-to-tag-lng-cargoes-with-ghg-emissions-data)

**MiQ**
- MiQ launched an LNG supply-chain GHG certification and registry in Mar 2023 — [Cyprus Shipping News](https://cyprusshippingnews.com/2023/03/29/miq-launches-worlds-first-comprehensive-greenhouse-gas-certification-and-registry-for-lng/)
- MiQ's open-source LNG **Supply Chain Protocol** (Mar 2024) uses LCA principles, accepts certified and uncertified data with quality ranking, and covers seven segments from US producer to landed Europe. In Mar 2024 MiQ certified about 22 Bcf/d (~20% of US production), with only a 1–5 cent premium because certificates were in surplus — [Hellenic Shipping News, 22 Mar 2024](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/miq-targets-lng-buyers-amid-oversupplied-us-certified-gas-market/)
- Grain LNG (UK) received grade "B" in Oct 2024, the first LNG terminal certified by MiQ. Certificates are issued to a producer account, transferred, and retired by end users with a retirement statement — [National Grid](https://www.nationalgrid.com/media-centre/press-releases/grain-lng-marks-world-first-auditing-methane-emissions); [LNG Industry](https://www.lngindustry.com/liquid-natural-gas/10102024/grain-lng-receives-certificate-from-miq/)

**Japan/Korea: CLEAN**
- CLEAN (Coalition for LNG Emission Abatement toward Net-Zero) was launched in 2023 by **JERA and KOGAS**, i.e. it is company-led, with government backing. Buyers send questionnaires to suppliers, and **JOGMEC** aggregates the responses and publishes annual reports on best practice — [FoE Japan, Aug 2025](https://foejapan.org/en/issue/20250823/25455/); [OGCI library (annual report)](https://www.ogci.com/methane-library-item/coalition-for-lng-emission-abatement-toward-net-zero-annual-report/)
- According to FoE, the annual report saw responses from 16 of 20 LNG projects (stated as ">80%", though 16/20 is exactly 80%), and only 7 of 20 (~35%) responded on methane emissions — [FoE Japan briefing PDF](https://foejapan.org/wpcms/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/FoE-Japan.-Is-CLEAN-Clean.-EN.pdf)
- By Oct 2024, 22 Japanese utilities and trading houses had joined, including all major power utilities, Mitsubishi and Mitsui — [BNN Bloomberg, Oct 2024](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/2024/10/04/japan-companies-unite-to-cut-methane-emissions-from-lng-supply)
- A 2023 multi-government statement (US, Japan, Korea, Australia, EU) backed moving toward methane and intensity data collection at **cargo, portfolio and operator level** (attribution from search summary) — [METI 2023 statement PDF](https://www.meti.go.jp/press/2023/07/20230719001/20230719001-2.pdf)
- June 2025 joint statement by METI, JOGMEC, IEA, UNEP IMEO, GIIGNL, EDF and MiQ on accelerating LNG supply-chain emission cuts, with JOGMEC as CLEAN secretariat (from search summary; PDF 403 on fetch) — [METI June 2025](https://www.meti.go.jp/press/2025/06/20250623002/20250623002-d.pdf)
- Critics (Oilfield Witness/FoE Japan/OCI, Feb 2025) filmed methane emissions at JERA's Futtsu LNG terminal and Goi plant — [FoE Japan](https://foejapan.org/en/issue/20250730/25186/)

### Inferences
- **Data sources a per-cargo GHG statement needs:** upstream producer methane (OGMP 2.0 L4/L5 site-level, MiQ grades, measurement-reconciled inventories), gathering/processing/pipeline segments (often defaults), liquefaction plant fuel, flare and vent data (plant historian, EPA Subpart W / NGER-type inventories), shipping (voyage fuel gas, GCU, slip factors, EU MRV data) and regas (terminal energy use, fugitives). Allocating these to *each cargo* needs the CTS energy figure as the denominator, so custody transfer data and emissions data converge.
- The one-year EUMR delay (if adopted) moves hard deadlines to ~2028, but the direction of travel (equivalence → intensity → cap) is unchanged. Buyers will keep collecting data under contract clauses, so 2026–2028 is a build-out window for tooling.
- Uncertainty around formats (templates not yet notified, verification bodies not yet accredited) favours flexible, user-configurable tools over rigid SaaS templates.

### Gaps
- The **"Statement of GHG Emissions (SGE)"** methodology used in Pavilion Energy (Singapore) SPAs with QatarEnergy, Chevron and others was not retrieved (search budget exhausted). Background, unverified here: Pavilion developed an SGE methodology requiring an independently verified per-cargo GHG statement from wellhead to discharge port.
- **Tokyo Gas and KOGAS-specific contract requirements**, and the content of the CLEAN questionnaire, were not found in accessible sources.
- Formal adoption status of the Oct 2026 EUMR delay, the exact new dates and the Annex IX data fields still need checking against the Official Journal and Commission pages.
- No source checked whether MiQ certificate issuance for LNG cargoes grew in 2025–2026. The figures above date from Mar 2024 and Oct 2024.

## 4. LNG market trends 2025–2026: supply wave, demand regions, terminals/cargoes, FSRUs

### Takeaway
2025 was a record year: GIIGNL counts 428 Mt of imports (+5%), IGU 437 Mt (+6.3%) and Shell 422 Mt. US exports drove it, alongside a +26–28 Mt rebound in European imports and lower Chinese buying. Liquefaction capacity reached ~524 MTPA and regas ~1,247 MTPA, with a record 9 FIDs (>90 bcm/y). The "supply wave" expected in 2026 (>7% growth) was disrupted by the **Strait of Hormuz closure from late Feb/early Mar 2026**, which shut in about 20% of global LNG supply (Qatar/UAE). North American start-ups (Golden Pass, Plaquemines, Corpus Christi Stage 3, LNG Canada) partly offset it.

### Inferences
- **Approximate cargo count (researcher's estimate, not sourced):** 428–437 Mt at roughly 65–70 kt per conventional cargo is about **6,000–6,700 large-scale cargoes per year** in 2025. - More than 250 regas terminals and ~50 FSRUs (count unverified; see Gaps) plus 524 MTPA of liquefaction at 20+ exporting countries means a fragmented user base: many sites with small measurement, scheduling and compliance teams. That fits per-seat desktop tools.
- The 2026 Hormuz shock raised spot activity, rerouting and portfolio swaps (e.g., QatarEnergy buying US cargoes; see §7). Each swap complicates traceability of cargo origin and emissions under EUMR, adding to data-processing demand.

## 5. Existing software tools for LNG operations and cargo management, and gaps; prevalence of spreadsheets

### Takeaway
The tool stack is layered. **Ship CTS / tank gauging:** Kongsberg K-Gauge, Emerson Rosemount, and others auto-generate CTS reports. **Terminal and hydrocarbon accounting, ADP and scheduling:** Quorum Energy Components / QLNG, which claims >50% of world LNG. **ETRM:** the ION family (Endur, Allegro, RightAngle, Triple Point, Aspect) and Molecule. **Shipping/voyage and emissions** tools form a further layer. Independent sources and vendors alike describe heavy, legacy **spreadsheet** use for scheduling, reconciliation and reporting. No product was found that combines custody transfer recalculation, ship-vs-shore reconciliation and per-cargo emissions statements in one auditable, offline tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Kongsberg K-Gauge CTS:** on-board system that logs level, temperature and pressure, computes volumes and automatically generates and prints the CTS loading/unloading report described as "the official document used for purchasing the gas". It claims approval by all major class societies (vendor claim) — [Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/maritime/products/tank-gauging-and-measurment-systems/custody-transfer-system)
- **Emerson Rosemount marine CTS:** dual-radar gauging with a single still pipe — [Emerson](https://emerson.com/cs-cz/industries/automation/marine/marine-systems-solution/custody-transfer-systems). Emerson also markets terminal management software, arguing against legacy spreadsheets — [Emerson Automation Experts](https://www.emersonautomationexperts.com/2024/industry/oil-gas/software-is-the-solution-for-a-more-efficient-lng-value-chain/)
- **Yokogawa:** automatic custody transfer for unmanned small-scale LNG stations, using CSV dispense logs and daily report files — [Yokogawa](https://yokogawa.com/tw/library/resources/application-notes/automatic-custody-transfer-system-for-unmanned-lng-station)
- **Quorum Energy Components (EC) Cargo Management:** a module within Quorum's hydrocarbon accounting platform that adds scheduling, decision support and optimisation. It covers portfolio-wide or terminal-specific **ADP** and short-term delivery schedules (SDS), berth allocation, KPI scenario analysis, and customer nominations and change requests at import terminals — [Quorum terminal management](https://www.quorumsoftware.com/solutions/lng-distribution/import-export-terminal-management/); [EC Cargo Management overview](https://resources.quorumsoftware.com/overviews/energy-components-cargo-management); [Quorum blog](https://resources.quorumsoftware.com/blog/5-ways-energy-components-supports-import-and-export-terminal-users)
- Quorum claims "**more than 50% of the world's LNG** is managed in solutions provided by Quorum Software" (vendor self-claim). Its **QLNG** product covers terminal customer contracts, vessel scheduling, redelivery nominations, inventory and back-office accounting — [Quorum](https://www.quorumsoftware.com/solutions/lng-distribution/import-export-terminal-management/); [NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/?p=39072)
- A Quorum-authored case study describes **Cheniere replacing spreadsheet-based LNG cargo processes** with integrated software, citing compliance with API, GPA and AGA measurement standards (vendor source; from search summary) — [Emerson/Quorum search results](https://www.gasprocessingnews.com/articles/2024/02/navigating-the-complex-lng-value-chain)
- **ETRM:** ION Endur and ION Allegro target large desks at very high cost and rollout effort. Molecule (a competitor) claims about **60% of the ETRM market** is now served by ION products (OpenLink Endur, RightAngle, Triple Point CXL, Aspect, Allegro Horizon). Molecule, a cloud ETRM, costs roughly $150k–$250k/yr per one source. **No source confirmed LNG cargo or vessel modules** for these vendors — [Molecule blog](https://molecule.io/blog/ion-allegro/); [OilPriceAPI ETRM overview](https://www.oilpriceapi.com/blog/etrm-software-features-costs-use-cases); [Allegro](https://allegrodevelopment.com)
- **Spreadsheets in scheduling:** a US patent on LNG ship-schedule optimisation states that "many conventional LNG projects tend to use **simple spreadsheets** for scheduling ships", populated manually with no optimisation — [USPTO 10878349](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10878349)
- Academic ADP and inventory-routing optimisation research (MIP, constraint programming, rolling-horizon heuristics) exists, but models are simplified and scale poorly — [Optimization Online](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/4211.pdf)
- **Spreadsheets in terminal accounting:** "much of this data is entered manually into complex spreadsheets that were written years ago by a small group of experienced users… designed for much simpler terminal operations". Errors propagate through macros into reports and audit trails, which "could result in fines or cancellation of contracts" — [Emerson, Gas Processing & LNG, Mar 2024](https://www.gasprocessingnews.com/articles/2024/02/navigating-the-complex-lng-value-chain)
- **Emissions:** supplier-run LCA models (Cheniere CE Tags), MiQ's registry and open-source supply-chain protocol, and verification by SGS (ISO 14064-3) — [Cheniere](https://www.cheniere.com/our-responsibility/climate/ce-tags); [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/miq-targets-lng-buyers-amid-oversupplied-us-certified-gas-market/)

### Inferences
- **Gap 1, independent recalculation:** CTS systems compute and print, and TMS/accounting systems store, but surveyors, buyers' measurement departments and auditors still need to *recalculate independently* with different standard editions and reference conditions, and to compare ship vs shore. That work typically happens in Excel (inferred from the spreadsheet evidence above).
- **Gap 2, emissions-per-cargo assembly:** no vendor product was found that ingests CTS energy figures, voyage fuel and slip data, and supplier intensity data and produces a verifiable per-cargo GHG or EUMR statement. Current approaches are supplier LCA models and certificate registries.
- **Gap 3, cost and accessibility:** enterprise systems (Quorum, ION) are priced and scoped for large operators and portfolio players. Mid-sized buyers, new importers (Bahrain, Senegal, Egypt, Philippines, Vietnam, etc.), FSRU operators, surveyors and consultants are likely under-served.
- **Gap 4, offline/air-gapped needs:** on-board and terminal OT environments and confidential commercial data favour desktop or offline tools. This is inferred and not sourced.

### Gaps
- **Honeywell, AVEVA, Siemens, ABB terminal-management/LNG offerings**, Cargill's internal tools, Veson IMOS/Dataloy/Marcura laytime tools and Kpler/Vortexa cargo-tracking data were not covered (search budget exhausted).
- No independent survey quantifying spreadsheet prevalence in LNG operations was found. The evidence is vendor articles and a patent statement.
- No practitioner forum or LinkedIn evidence was gathered.

## 6. Pain points: data reconciliation (ship vs shore, disputes, units/reference conditions), reporting formats, auditability

### Takeaway
The recurring pain points are:
- ship-vs-shore and load-vs-discharge discrepancies with a contested hierarchy of evidence;
- inconsistent reference conditions and unit conventions (MMBtu vs kWh/GJ, 25/0 vs 15/15 vs 60°F) and conversion factors;
- mixing ISO and GPA calculation bases;
- static tank measurement with no proven dynamic metering for LNG;
- GC calibration and sampling representativeness;
- BOG and heel accounting;
- manual, macro-laden spreadsheets with weak audit trails;
- a new, poorly defined emissions-data layer (EUMR templates and verification still pending).

### Cited Findings
- Mixing ISO and GPA reference standards is discouraged by the handbook. Coriolis and ultrasonic meters are not conventional for LNG custody transfer because proving methods are lacking — [sea-man.org](https://sea-man.org/lng-measurement.html)
- Emerson practitioners describe LNG tanker custody-transfer uncertainty as a significant fiscal risk and propose check metering and proving to validate transfer discrepancies — [Emerson Exchange 365](https://emersonexchange365.com/industries/oilandgas/f/discussions-questions/3948/dynamic-measurement-solutions-in-lng-custody-transfer)
- "The usage of different reference conditions, and the associated misunderstanding, for both LNG and displaced gas can have financial consequences for the buyer or seller of LNG" (excerpt from search results; attribution to this page not individually verified) — [sea-man.org GCV Q&A](https://sea-man.org/qa/what-is-gcv)
- Evidence of divergent conventions: EU terminals use 25 °C combustion / 0 °C metering and kWh, while international trade quotes MMBtu. Conversion factors differ (53.38 vs 52.14 MMBtu/t), and Platts had to standardise MMBtu→GJ at 1.055056 in Aug 2026 — [Gasum Pori](https://www.gasum.com/globalassets/files/pdf/terminals/pori/pori-terminal-rules-annex-1-lng-quality-specifications.pdf); [Konkurentsiamet](https://www.konkurentsiamet.ee/sites/default/files/konsultatsioonid/annex_13_-_conversion_table_-_30_sep_2022.pdf); [henergy](https://henergy.com/conversion); [Platts](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/pricing-benchmarks/our-methodology/subscriber-notes/082426-platts-standardizes-mmbtu-gig-conversion-factor)
- GC quality issues: single-point (type 2) calibration can carry non-linearity errors, which is why ISO 10723 multi-point performance evaluation is "best practice". The 6th edition demands CRM-grade calibration gas — [EffecTech 5th ed.](https://www.effectech.co.uk/giignl-lng-custody-transfer-handbook-5th-edition-performance-evaluations-specified-as-best-practice/); [EffecTech 6th ed.](https://www.effectech.co.uk/?p=2947)
- Sampling representativeness is "key to ensuring regulatory compliance and demonstrating fulfilment of your contractual obligations" (SGS), and SGS sells independent verification of terminal samplers — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/lng-portable-sampler-and-testing-rig)
- General liquid-cargo shortage causes include incorrect shore tank calibration, wrong line-displacement quantities and meter calibration errors. Masters should issue Letters of Protest — [Britannia P&I, Apr 2026](https://britanniapandi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Britannia-Loss-Prevention-Guidance-Liquid-Cargo-Shortages-1.pdf)
- Contracts set a measurement **order of preference**: custody transfer meters, then shore tanks, then vessel gauging with an experience factor, then renegotiation (generic sample clause) — [Law Insider vessel clause](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/vessel/_9)
- Terminal data pain points: manual spreadsheet entry, error propagation through macros into audit trails, contract data siloed from movement spreadsheets, multi-tank unloading with several custody owners, quality and GCV per tank, regas allocation, BOG, imbalance calculations, periodic and on-demand reports to customers and pipeline owners, and demurrage calculation — [Emerson, Gas Processing & LNG](https://www.gasprocessingnews.com/articles/2024/02/navigating-the-complex-lng-value-chain)
- BOG and charterparty disputes over calculation basis, exclusions, reliquefaction and evidence (right to sample) — [Gard, Nov 2025](https://www.gard.no/en/insights/when-cargo-becomes-fuel-complexities-of-boil-off-and-reliquefaction-in-lng)
- Emissions-data pain: EUMR implementing acts and templates were not yet notified in June 2026, competent authorities had not all been nominated, verifier pools are limited and producer-level data is unavailable for portfolio/FOB US LNG — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/06/Uncertain-times-for-the-EU-Methane-Regulation); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/eu-methane-rules-impact-global-lng-exporters)
- Supplier emissions figures are estimates. Cheniere reserves the right to change its method without notice and states no legal requirement governs the estimation, which makes comparison across suppliers hard — [Cheniere CE Tags](https://www.cheniere.com/our-responsibility/climate/ce-tags)

### Inferences
- **Persona pain map (inferred):**
  - *Terminal measurement and accounting staff:* multi-owner tank allocation, GCV per tank, BOG/send-out reconciliation, monthly customer statements, and audit trail integrity.
  - *Commercial/scheduling staff:* ADP/SDS changes, nominations, laytime/demurrage time sheets, invoices built on surveyor energy figures, unit conversions across SPAs, and a new need to attach emissions data to cargoes.
  - *Surveyors:* recalculating from CTS printouts and GC reports, checking calibration certificate validity, issuing signed reports, and handling ship-vs-shore and B/L vs outturn discrepancy explanations.
  - *Ship operators:* BOG guarantee calculations, EU MRV/ETS methane, FuelEU.
- A workbench that offers deterministic, versioned calculations (standard edition + reference conditions + factor set recorded with each result), side-by-side ship/shore comparison and exportable audit packs maps directly onto these pains.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on dispute frequency, typical ship-vs-shore variance (%) or financial losses from reconciliation errors in LNG.
- No practitioner-forum or LinkedIn testimony was collected (search budget exhausted).
- No source on how many terminals still rely on spreadsheets versus a TMS.

## 7. Regional differences: Qatar/GCC, US Gulf Coast, Europe, Asia (Japan, Korea, China, India), Africa, Australia, Canada

### Takeaway
Regions differ in commercial terms (Qatar/Australia DES long-term vs US FOB portfolio), measurement conventions (EU 25/0 kWh vs MMBtu elsewhere) and regulatory pull. The EU has the EUMR, EU ETS for shipping and FuelEU. Japan and Korea work through the voluntary CLEAN questionnaires, the US through FERC quality rules, MiQ and supplier tags. 2025–2026 events reshaped flows: the US surge, the European rebound, China's decline, Hormuz/Ras Laffan outages in 2026, new exporters (Canada, Mauritania/Senegal), the Mozambique LNG restart, and new importers (Bahrain, Senegal, a growing Egypt).

### Cited Findings
**Qatar/GCC**
- QatarEnergy took Ras Laffan LNG offline on 2 Mar 2026 after a drone attack and declared force majeure to some buyers. It loaded its first cargo after force majeure in early March — [Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2795563-qatarenergy-issues-force-majeure-to-some-lng-buyers); [World Oil, 6 Mar 2026](https://admin.worldoil.com/news/2026/3/6/qatar-loads-first-lng-cargo-since-force-majeure/)

### Inferences
- **Europe** is the strongest regulatory pull for per-cargo methane and MRV data (EUMR plus ETS/FuelEU), and its terminals report in kWh at 25/0. Conversion to and from MMBtu-based SPAs is a constant friction point.
- **US Gulf Coast** exporters and portfolio traders face the hardest traceability problem (FOB, many producers) and hold the most supplier-side emissions modelling (CE Tags, MiQ). Tools that merge certificate data and CTS energy per cargo are relevant here.
- **Japan/Korea** buyers rely on voluntary but structured supplier questionnaires (CLEAN). Demand there is for aggregation and benchmarking of supplier responses, plus GIIGNL-style cargo statements.
- **Qatar/GCC** long-term DES supply with in-house measurement was disrupted in 2026. Force majeure, replacement-cargo sourcing (US spot) and rerouting generate unusual reconciliation and documentation workloads (force majeure notices, substitute-cargo quality and energy differences).
- **Africa** (GTA, Nigeria, Angola, Mozambique from 2029, plus new importers like Egypt and Senegal) has a growing number of sites with thinner IT infrastructure, so offline desktop tools are plausibly attractive. This is inferred.
- **China/India** are price-sensitive, fast-growing terminal markets. India's 8th terminal shows the growing base of mid-sized operators.

- The **EU regulation phasing out Russian LNG imports** (with proof-of-origin documentation requirements for importers) was not verified this session. Background, unverified: the EU adopted rules in 2025–2026 banning Russian LNG under short-term contracts from 2026 and long-term contracts by 2027, requiring importers to show country of production. This would be another per-cargo documentation driver and should be checked.
